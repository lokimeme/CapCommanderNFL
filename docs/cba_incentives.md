# NFL Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) Incentive Guide

This guide details the 'Likely to be Earned' (LTBE) and 'Not Likely to be Earned' (NLTBE) mechanics.

## 1. Defining LTBE vs NLTBE
Contract incentives are categorized at the start of the league year based on the player's performance in the *previous* year.

### 1.1 LTBE (Likely To Be Earned)
*   **Definition:** An incentive where the player achieved the specified metric in the prior season.
*   **Cap Impact:** Hits the *current* year's cap immediately.
*   **Example:** A $1M bonus for 10 TDs. If the player had 10 TDs last year, the $1M counts against the current cap.

### 1.2 NLTBE (Not Likely To Be Earned)
*   **Definition:** An incentive where the player did *not* achieve the metric in the prior season.
*   **Cap Impact:** Does *not* hit the current cap.
*   **Adjustment:** If earned, it hits the *following* year's cap as a debit.

## 2. Year-End Reconciliation
Front offices must manage 'Cap Carryover' by predicting which NLTBEs will hit and which LTBEs will yield a credit.

### 2.1 The Incentive Credit
If an LTBE is *not* earned, the team receives a cap credit in the following league year (e.g., $1M extra space).

### 2.2 The Incentive Debit
If an NLTBE is earned, the team receives a cap debit (less space) in the following year.

## 3. Strategic Use of Incentives
Teams in 'Cap Hell' often use heavy NLTBE structures to push current-year costs into the future, essentially taking a 'loan' against next year's cap.
